Overview

SQLite, SQL and Python turn a large public app-market dataset into a documented, analysis-ready structure. Data quality is treated as an analytical result: suspicious values are flagged and retained rather than silently corrected.

The challenge

Mixed text, numeric, boolean and timestamp fields made manual review impractical. Missing links, invalid timestamps, pricing mismatches, non-positive file sizes and duplicate identifiers required consistent rules.

Method

From raw questions to reliable evidence.

01

Standardise

Clean text, coerce numeric fields, normalise booleans and parse timestamps to UTC.

02

Deduplicate

Remove exact duplicates and retain the most recently updated record for repeated App_Id values.

03

Flag

Create explicit Issue_* fields and Quality_Issue_Count instead of guessing ambiguous values.

04

Validate

Export CSV and SQLite outputs, then reconcile counts, identifiers, flags and pricing logic in SQL.

Decision log

The choices I made—and why they matter.

  • Preserve uncertain records with issue flags instead of aggressive imputation.
  • Derive Free_By_Price while retaining the original Free field for comparison.
  • Keep the most recently updated record when App_Id is duplicated.
  • Document reproducible paths while keeping large raw and processed data out of the repository.

Outcomes

What the evidence produced.

  • Built an analysis-ready table containing 1,229,886 apps.
  • Confirmed a free-app majority: 1,127,384 free versus 102,502 paid apps.
  • Identified Games as the largest category and created reusable market views.
